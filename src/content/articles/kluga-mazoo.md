November 6, 2024

I just left the island of Kluga-mazoo,
The one with the goblins and pandemic stew.

I left pretty quickly, after quite a long stay, 
with a mask, and some rations, and tempered dismay.

As I walked toward the ship, and prepared to set sail, 
Belligerent goblins were hot on my tail.

They chased me with focused and aimless pursuit,
With spears and their torches, intending to loot.

I escaped, thank goodness, my journey on track,
And I knew in my mind I would never go back.

One goblin yelled when I was just out of reach,
He grunted and drooled, then he pooped on the beach.

So then out to sea, uncharted my course,
Decisive, determined, and without remorse.

The seas that I sailed were uncertain, you see,
But the land that I left was just not for me.

Pandemics and protests and Twitter affronts,
Nights soaked in pee-pee and political stunts.

Those goblins were nasty, but some wore fine linen,
Yet others were ruthless and feasted on women.

I sought something different deep down in my heart,
And sailing that ship was a good place to start.

The seas were not perfect and there wasn’t a map,
But I’d been there before and steered clear of the traps.

The first hundred days were a pleasant affair,
I began to feel normal and grow out my hair.

But then I got scared or maybe just bored,
I’d already eaten all the food I had stored.

I was ready for comfort and coconut cream,
I longed for a sauna and therapy steam.

I stopped on an island that seemed to be sound,
But certainty there was yet to be found.

I wandered and journeyed and trudged ‘cross the land,
through jungles of debt and inflation quicksand.

I came on a village that seemed nice enough,
The people were normal and had lots of stuff.

In search of the truth, I saw the town sage,
The tribe’s supreme leader, despite his old age.

I needed some wisdom, with mine to compare,
But when the man spoke, he wasn’t all there.

So what can I do? I decided to see, 
As choice and our spirit is what makes us free.

I boarded my boat without much event,
I dropped down the sails and then off I went.

Three months on the water, I sailed day and night,
I knew I had purpose, my future was bright.

When a storm formed ahead, I was left with a choice: 
To trust my new path or hear an old voice.

Sail into the storm and chart a new path? 
Or take a tried course and forgo all that math?

As I then turned the ship away from the storm,
Yuge echoes of “Bigly” started to form.

I figured these voices were all in my head,
So I yawned and spaced out, then went straight to bed.

I woke up mid-morning, with a splitting headache,
I came up on deck and saw a landscape.

The air was quite hazy the scenery bland,
Both glowed the dull hues of orange spray tan.

My headache was growing, my future was stark,
‘Twas nothing to do except disembark.

So I gathered my things to see what was new,
And stepped on the beach of Kluga-mazoo.
