Economists have identified two trends that are heating up quickly, promising to broaden class divides and ignite a socioeconomic dumpster fire.

New models released by the Bureau of Reality Adjustment indicate that income for lower and middle tier earners is trending towards complete oblivion, while wealth accumulation at the top has never been stronger.

The emerging norm, which experts now refer to as the F-U economy, shows that earners with average incomes are fading into a long-term “F’d” pattern, indicating that consumer confidence has stagnated in, well, everything. 


Further analysis shows that while wealth inequality took a momentary dip, it has lately surged upward into an opening parabola. The accelerated shift points to a massive hoarding of assets while bankrupting the broader population in the form of astronomical rents, pricey healthcare, and outrageously expensive food.


A strikingly similar F-U progression appears when analyzing home ownership opportunities for first time buyers who are pretty certain that they will never own a home. 






 

Looking ahead, the F-U curve will become an increasingly useful tool as we try to understand the impossible living costs in urban centers, crisis-level insurance premiums, and other cartoonishly expensive basic goods. 

Some experts warn that, if F-U trends continue, the economy will spontaneously burst into a scatter plot with indiscernible variables and extreme outliers. 


Then we’re all F’d.
