It has been a good run, but after 52 years of public service, Wilburn has decided to retire. 

Contrary to popular belief, this loyal federal employee was not slain in 1979 by the fed and its fierce squad of dragon hunters—he has been working diligently and quietly under federal benefits since 1974. 

“It’s time” Wilburn said in a brief interview with The Mantis—"A new generation of inflaters is at the helm and it is time for me to step aside”

It’s not so much that the job is obsolete, he says. Technology and other market enablers have empowered individuals to make their own personalized inflationary creatures which are far more terrifying than any other overused metaphor. 

E-commerce powered by AI has played a part, Wilburn observed.  Hyper-personalization allows each individual to breathe fire on their own savings and personal credit limits. Click-buy relationships between consumers and their things helps to stabilize price escalation, with the mutual benefit of scorching natural resources. 
Innovative mechanisms like supply-side subsidies set the price of all commodities, ensuring that, once inflation is trending upward, it is here to stay.

In his newfound spare time, Wilburn plans to catch up on some reading, reconnect with old friends, and find general equilibrium in his day-to day.

He also looks forward to weekly bingo during game theory night at his community center, embracing the marginal utility of his golf game, and trying some new fishing spots with bounded rationality.
