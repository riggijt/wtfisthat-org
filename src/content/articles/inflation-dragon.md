It’s been a good run, but after a long career in public service, the inflationary dragon has decided to retire. 

Contrary to popular belief, Wilburn was not slain by the Federal Reserve and its elite squad of dragon hunters in 1979.

According to the Consumer Price Index and other obvious indicators, Wilburn has worked diligently under federal benefits since 1974.  As a government employee, he’s quietly and effectively eroded the purchasing power of most fiat currencies.  

Lately, however, Wilburn has been sidelined by emerging inflationary innovations— a shift that ultimately prompted his retirement.

“It’s time” Wilburn said in a brief interview with The Mantis. "A new generation of inflaters is at the helm, and it’s my duty now to step aside”

“It’s not that inflation is obsolete,” he said. “Technology and other modern market systems have empowered individuals to make their own personalized inflationary creatures, many of which are far more terrifying than overused metaphors like me.”

Economists agree with Wilburn’s assessment. 

Hyper-personalized advertising empowers individuals to breathe fire directly onto their own savings accounts and personal credit limits.  Meanwhile, algorithmically optimized impulse buying has largely automated the inflation process, while simultaneously scorching natural resources.


Wilburn also expressed admiration for supply-side subsidies and similarly innovative mechanisms which ensure that once inflation trends upward, it is here to stay.


In retirement, Wilburn plans to catch up on some reading, reconnect with old friends, and find general equilibrium in his day-to-day.  He looks forward to weekly game theory night at his community center, embracing the marginal utility of his golf game, and trying some new fishing spots with bounded rationality.


When asked whether he was worried about inflation continuing upwards, Wilburn chuckled.


"No," he said. "I think they're going to be just fine without me."
